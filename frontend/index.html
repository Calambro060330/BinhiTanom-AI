About the study

Why a seed-identifying garden guide?

Household vegetable gardening can help families grow fresh food in yards, balconies, containers, and small home plots. But beginners often do not know which vegetable a seed belongs to, what soil to prepare, how much sunlight it needs, when to water, or which fertilizer to use. Unclear or overly general advice can lead to poor germination and wasted seeds.

This study develops a web-based prototype that links seed-image identification with bilingual gardening guidance. After a user uploads a seed photo, the system shows the predicted vegetable with a confidence score, then retrieves a reviewed guide covering soil, planting depth and spacing, sunlight, watering, fertilizer, container size, and harvest.

The guide is stored in a curated database, not generated by the image model, so every piece of advice can be checked and corrected.

Problem and objectives

What we want to find out

Household gardeners may struggle to identify vegetable seeds and to find clear, trustworthy growing instructions in their preferred language. The study asks:

  1. How accurately can a deep learning model classify images of the selected vegetable seed classes?
  2. What are the precision, recall, F1-score, and average prediction time on a held-out test set?
  3. How can the system present crop-specific guidance after a seed is identified?
  4. How understandable and usable do household gardeners find the English and Hiligaynon guides?
  5. What limitations arise from image quality, similar-looking seeds, dataset size, and translating agricultural terms?
General objectiveTo develop and evaluate a deep learning-based local vegetable seed identification and household gardening recommendation system with Hiligaynon language support for Iloilo Province.
01Collect and organize labeled images of selected household vegetable seeds.
02Develop an image classifier using a CNN or pretrained model.
03Build a database of reviewed gardening recommendations.
04Provide English and Hiligaynon guides with a language switch.
05Evaluate accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and prediction time.
06Evaluate usability and clarity of the bilingual guides with intended users.
07Document limitations and recommend improvements.

Transfer learning

A pretrained model is adapted to a small seed dataset, which needs less training than building a large model from scratch. Its performance is measured on the selected local classes, never assumed from other benchmarks.

Separate guide database

The predicted label is only a key. Horticultural advice comes from reviewed entries, so it is consistent and easy to update in both languages.

Honest confidence

The confidence score is a model output, not a guarantee. Predictions are informational and may be wrong.
